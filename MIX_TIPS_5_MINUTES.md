# Mix Tips: Fatt Mack "5 Minutes" breakdown (by the producer/engineer, IG Live)

Source: youtu.be/Halwb4DSLKU. These are the moves the engineer described on this song, not rules for every mix.

## How the beat was built
- Started from one melodic **loop** a producer sent. Everything else is built around that loop.
- **The 808 is the star:** big, distorted and "flubby." That's the vibe of the song.
- A **kick** was layered on top only for the punch/transient. The engineer cut a lot of the kick's low end so it wouldn't fight the 808.
- Clap or snare, then hi-hats, then percussion and an open hat panned around for stereo width.
- Loud, slightly smashed and clipped overall.

## Beat / instrumental
- Send the whole beat to one bus. EQ out some **mids around 1 kHz**, because that's where the rapper's voice lives, so the vocal has room.
- Widen the mids and highs a little, but keep the **low end mono**.
- A little clipping on the beat bus.

## 808 and kick
- 808: filter off extra top end and remove any noise.
- **Invisible sidechain trick:** send the kick to the sidechain of a dynamic EQ (they used Soothe) that ducks only the **808's low end under ~200 Hz** whenever the kick hits. It sounds tight without that pumping sidechain sound.
- Kick: cut excess lows, add top-end presence and click, then light clipping (Gold Clip) for loudness.
- Use **volume automation on the kick** to shorten its tail so it doesn't overlap the 808's sustain. This makes a big difference on club speakers.

## Clap, snare, hats
- Clap: just a light clip (about 3 dB) to control peaks.
- Hats: left untouched on this song. Usually a high-pass.
- Percs and open hat: mainly about stereo placement, using an imager to spread them out.

## Vocals (main)
- Record with the tone you want from the start, so the mix moves stay small.
- Tame harsh resonance around **600–900 Hz** (especially ~828 Hz on this song) and around **2 kHz**.
- Compressor plus saturation, then small SSL EQ cuts (about 1 dB at 300 Hz, 700 Hz and 1.2 kHz).
- De-esser, then Soothe for harshness and "digital" top end.
- High-pass at **70 Hz**.
- **Auto-Tune on each vocal track, not the master.** Setting: retune speed around **8**. "It's all about how you perform into the Auto-Tune."

## Adlibs
- Same chain as the mains, plus a **doubler** and a little **lo-fi** so they pop.
- Lots of reverb and delay, panned **wide**.

## Vocal bus
- Fairchild-style compressor, light.
- **Parallel compression:** send to an 1176 smashing hard (20:1) and blend it in underneath. It adds a "midrange wall" behind the vocal.
- **Ducking:** a dynamic EQ on the beat, triggered by the vocal, dips the beat's competing frequencies only while the rapper is talking.

## Whole mix / master
- Parallel sends of the whole mix: one heavy compressor, one top-end exciter, one blown-out tape saturation. Blend them in quietly for extra energy without eating headroom.
- Master: light Gold Clip, a little top-end boost, transient emphasis (more in the low end), wide mids for the adlibs, dynamic EQ for build-up, and a compressor grabbing **everything under ~80 Hz** to keep the low end from blowing out the master.
- Reverb: mostly a simple vocal reverb. A doubler plus flanger send together adds extra movement.

## Quick takeaways for my songs
1. Leave a hole around 1 kHz in the beat for my voice.
2. Sidechain only the 808's sub (under 200 Hz) to the kick.
3. Auto-Tune on each vocal track, retune about 8, and perform into it.
4. Parallel-compress the vocals hard and blend them in low.
5. Keep the low end mono and controlled on the master.
