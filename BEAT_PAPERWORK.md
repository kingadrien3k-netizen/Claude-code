## 1. Can I use these beats?

**Yes, for releasing music, including commercially. One gray area is explained below.**

### What vidIQ's Terms of Service say (vidiq.com/terms, read Nov 17, 2026)

> "As between you and vidIQ, you may use Content Artifacts you create through the Services for personal or commercial purposes, including in monetized and promotional content, subject to these Terms of Service, third-party rights, and applicable platform and vendor terms. vidIQ does not require attribution or an active subscription for continued use of Content Artifacts already generated."

What that means:
- I can use the beats commercially: songs, streaming and monetized videos.
- I don't have to credit vidIQ.
- I keep the right to use beats I already generated even if I cancel my vidIQ plan.

The same section also says:

> "Except for any rights expressly granted to you in these Terms of Service, vidIQ grants you no license or other right in or to any Content Artifact, and you may not rely on vidIQ to clear, license, or obtain rights in any Content Artifact on your behalf."

What that means: vidIQ lets me *use* the beats, but it doesn't promise I *own* them outright, and it doesn't guarantee that no one else has a claim.

### What DistroKid requires (support.distrokid.com, read Nov 17, 2026)

DistroKid accepts music made with AI tools if:
1. **I own 100% of the rights**, "including the legal right to distribute music created with any AI tools."
2. **No impersonation.** The track can't copy another artist's voice, likeness or identity.
3. **No mass-generated spam.** I can't flood the platforms with generic tracks.
4. **No infringement** of anyone else's rights.

Since April 2026, DistroKid's upload form also asks which parts of a song are AI-generated (lyrics, the music, all of the audio, or part of the audio) and shows that as AI credits on Spotify, Apple Music and YouTube.

### The gray area

- DistroKid says "own 100% of the rights." vidIQ says "you may use… for commercial purposes" but doesn't say I own them. Usually that's treated as enough to release, but it isn't a written ownership guarantee.
- Under current US law, music made entirely by AI generally can't be copyrighted. My **lyrics, vocals, melodies and the recorded song** are mine. The beat alone may not be protectable.
- **Before releasing, I should email vidIQ support** and ask: *"Can I distribute songs that use music I generated with vidIQ's AI music tool to Spotify and Apple Music through DistroKid, and do I hold the distribution rights?"* I'll save their written reply with this document.
