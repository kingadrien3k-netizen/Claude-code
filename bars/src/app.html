Finding the pocket

  1. Nod on every beat. The snare lands on beats 2 and 4 (the outlined dots).
  2. Aim your rhyme words at the snare. Ending a bar on beat 4 is what makes a rhyme hit.
  3. Keep each bar's syllable count close to the others. Big jumps make you rush or stall.
  4. Start a line a hair before beat 1. Rappers call that a pickup; it pulls the listener in.
  5. Record yourself on your phone, then listen back without the lyrics in front of you.

How to freestyle

  1. Don't stop. A weak bar delivered on beat beats a great bar that never comes.
  2. Think one bar ahead. While you say this line, pick the word that ends the next.
  3. Fill gaps with the room: describe what you see, what you're wearing, the time.
  4. Rhyme the drop word, then rhyme off of your own rhyme to chain three or four bars.
  5. Do five minutes a day. Freestyle is a muscle, and you build it in reps.

Why it works

A double entendre makes the listener hear the line twice: once the obvious way, then again when the second meaning clicks. That delay is what makes people rewind.

Set up the first meaning early in the bar, then let the context around the key word point at the second one. Save the word itself for the end of the bar so it lands on the snare.
