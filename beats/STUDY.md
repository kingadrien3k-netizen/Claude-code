## What they do (and what my AABB couplets were missing)
1. **Rhyme chains, not couplets.** They stay on ONE vowel sound for 6–20 bars, then switch.
   - Gunna "fukumean": ~20 bars all on "-urr" (splurge/curve/bird/third/word/heard/swerve/purge/nerves/perv/perm/turn/merge/learn/earn/worm), then a long "-illy / -illin" chain (pretty/ceiling/million/really/billion/city/villains/feeling/chillin/killing).
   - Gunna "HIM ALL ALONG": long "-o" chain (gold/stones/home/prolong/goals/close/clothes/old/holes/folk/grow/coast).
   - Jay Montana "That's Law": "-ur" chain (purse/shirt/hurt/words/slur/serve/heard), then "-o" (mode/show/road/control/stole).
2. **Echo ad-libs.** Repeat the last word or two of the bar in parentheses: "(Gold)", "(In stones)", "(Dress my home)". Fills the pocket and makes it catchy.
3. **Hooks rhyme in the middle AND the end of every line.** "Drip Too Hard": "drip too hard, don't stand too close" / "bad lil' vibe, she been on my mind" / "do this all the time, this ain't no surprise" — vibe/mind/time/surprise inside, wave/paid/slayed/made at the ends. Hook gets run twice back to back.
4. **Repeated line openers (anaphora).** Lil Tony "Watch The Moon": "I'm just tryna..." on line after line; Jay Montana: "Why you ..." x4; Gunna: "Every other night...".
5. **Multi-syllable end rhymes.** Rylo "Set Me Free": hurt me / birthed me / perky / search me. Rylo "Walk": ransom / dancers / hands on / pants on; horrible / poured it up / boarded up.
6. **Specific details over generic lines.** Car models, gun models, prices, cities, brands, real people. Not "I came from the bottom".
7. **Truth punchlines.** Plain, hard, honest one-liners ("woman can't make me do nothing if she ain't birthed me"), not clichés.
8. **Short songs.** Most of their 1M+ hits are 2:00–3:15 with one or two verses; BabyStayDown and Lil Tony sit around 2:00–2:45.

## Rules for writing nocap lyrics from now on
- Use rhyme chains (6+ bars on one vowel, then switch). No more AABB nursery couplets.
- Echo ad-libs in () at the end of most bars.
- Hook: every line rhymes in the middle and at the end; 4 lines, run twice.
- At least one stretch of repeated line openers per verse.
- Land 2-syllable end rhymes where possible.
- Concrete details, no storytelling, no clichés. Never copy their lines — techniques only.
